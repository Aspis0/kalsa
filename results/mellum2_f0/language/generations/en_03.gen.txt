The practical difference between **TCP** and **UDP** lies in how they handle data transmission, reliability, and ordering. Here's a breakdown:

---

### **TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)**
- **Reliable**: Ensures data arrives intact and in order.
- **Connection-oriented**: Establishes a connection before sending data.
- **Error-checking**: Uses acknowledgments and retransmissions.
- **Flow control**: Adjusts data rate based on receiver capacity.

**Example Workload**:  
**Web Browsing (HTTP/HTTPS)**  
When you load a webpage, TCP ensures that all HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and image files are received correctly and in the right order. If a packet is lost, TCP retransmits it. This is critical for a seamless user experience.

---

### **UDP (User Datagram Protocol)**
- **Unreliable**: No guarantee of delivery, order, or error checking.
- **Connectionless**: Sends data without establishing a connection.
- **Fast**: Minimal overhead, no retransmissions.
- **No flow control**: Sends data as fast as possible.

**Example Workload**:  
**Online Gaming (e.g., multiplayer FPS games)**  
In fast-paced games, UDP is preferred because low latency is more important than perfect accuracy. A slight delay in receiving a packet is acceptable, but retransmissions would cause lag. UDP sends data quickly, even if some packets are lost.

---
