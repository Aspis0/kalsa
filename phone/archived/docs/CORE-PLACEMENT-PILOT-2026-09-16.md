# Core-placement pilot — the little-cluster lever fails on latency (2026-09-16)

Fase 2b asked whether an energy-objective policy over application-level levers is
worth building at all, with core placement as the first candidate lever. Published
prior art (MNN-AECS, arXiv 2506.19884) reports about 23 % less decode energy by
running decode on low-power cores **at no slowdown**.

The pilot tested exactly that on the Jelly Star: ABBA-ordered, temperature-gated,
taskset masks proven in-band. It answers the question in the negative, on the
latency axis, by a margin no energy result could recover.

## What is robust

**Restricting decode to the six A55 cores costs 5.0–5.3x decode time.** Read
directly from the per-rep speed lines (verified independently of the instrument's
report): `a76_t2` 3.7/3.7/3.7 and 3.7/3.7/3.7 t/s against `a55_t2` 0.8/0.7/0.7 and
0.7/0.7/0.7 t/s, twelve reps.

The placement is proven, not assumed: the wrapped process prints its own
`Cpus_allowed_list` — 0-5 in the A55 arms, 6-7 in the A76 arms — and the per-CPU
clocks corroborate it (on the A55 arms cpu0-5 sit at ~1.99 GHz while cpu6-7 idle
at ~0.76 GHz, and the reverse on the A76 arms). A silently ignored mask would have
shown 0-7 everywhere; it did not.

Against the owner's reading rule — accept up to +25 % decode time for at least 15 %
less joules per token — the arm reports **+402 % decode time** for −39 % J/token.
The rule fails by a factor of about sixteen on its latency term, before the energy
term is even considered.

The six-thread variant cannot rescue it. To enter the rule's latency window an
`a55_t6` arm would need roughly 0.8x the A76 throughput, i.e. about 3.0 t/s; from
0.7 t/s at two threads that requires a 4.3x speedup from 3x the threads, which
decode scaling does not deliver. That is a bound from the measured points, not an
assumption.

## What is not trustworthy, and why

The instrument marked all four blocks **UNINTERPRETABLE**, correctly. Two defects,
both more consequential than the headline:

- **The device dozes during the arms.** Every arm records
  `mWakefulness=Awake` at the start and `mWakefulness=Dozing` at the end. The A55
  arms last ~350 s against the A76 arms' ~69 s, so the doze consumes a far larger
  share of them, and a dozing SoC draws less power. This biases the slow arm toward
  a flatteringly low J/token. It is the same shape of error as a coverage
  asymmetry: the arm that runs longer gets credited for idling.
- **The sampler's cadence degrades exactly on the slow arms**: median 2.46–2.53 s
  and max 4.1–4.5 s on the A55 stems against a 1.04 s median on the A76 stems.
  Every rep of every arm carries the cadence warning.

The combination is visible in the numbers: the A55 decode buckets imply 0.20–0.23 W
over 350 s, below this session's own screen-awake idle floor of **0.506 W**. An
inference workload cannot draw less than idle, so the −39 % is not a measurement of
the lever; it is a measurement of the doze.

The same-arm ABBA order step says the same thing: `a55_t2` reads 0.448 J/token in
its early position against 0.321 in its late one, −28.5 %, on the same arm. The A76
arm's step is +2.2 %.

## What this means for Fase 2b

The lever that published prior art is built around does not transfer to llama.cpp
on this SoC. MNN-AECS's claim depends on low-power cores costing *no* throughput;
here they cost 5x. The negative lands on latency, which is the very axis the claim
needs.

**The energy-objective policy should not be built on core placement.** What remains
of 2b is a latency and thermal question, not an energy one — and that should be
recorded as the outcome, not worked around.

## Status of the instrument

`scripts/device-energy-sweep.sh` is current after commit `40ca30d`: it re-arms
keep-awake and wakes the display before every arm, records and checks the screen
state at the arm's start and end, and marks a block **UNINTERPRETABLE** when doze
is observed during the arm. The pilot's own numbers were produced before
`40ca30d` and remain invalidated by the doze observed in that run; this fix does
not retroactively make them usable.

The cadence degradation may share the doze's root cause rather than load, but
that remains unverified.
