Station No. 4 of the Polish General Staff (Ekspozytura Nr 4 Oddziału II Sztabu Głównego), also known as Station No. 4 in Katowice or Branch No. 4, was a regional operational military intelligence and counterintelligence station of the Second Polish Republic, active from 1923 until the outbreak of World War II in September 1939.
Subordinate directly to Bureau "West" (Referat Zachód) within Department IIa (Offensive Intelligence) of the Second Department of Polish General Staff (colloquially known as the Dwójka), the station was responsible for conducting tactical and operational reconnaissance—termed in interwar Polish military doctrine as "shallow intelligence" (wywiad płytki)—across an operational sector extending 100 to 150 kilometres into foreign territory, covering the southern frontier from Rawicz to the Carpathian Mountains.
Originally established in Kraków, the station was relocated in September 1930 to Katowice in the border region of Upper Silesia to provide immediate observation of Weimar Germany and later Nazi Germany. Its reconnaissance area encompassed the German military districts in Silesia (both Lower Silesia and Oppeln Silesia), parts of Bavaria, and following the 1938 Anschluss, Austria and partitioned Czechoslovakia (primarily the puppet Slovak Republic).
Between 1935 and 1939, under the leadership of Major Kazimierz Szpądrowski, the station developed one of the most effective human intelligence (HUMINT) networks operated by Polish intelligence in central Europe, expanding its network in Germany to dozens of recruited agents, executing deception and penetration games against the Gestapo in Bytom, and identifying covert German fortification lines along the Oder River. Upon the outbreak of war in September 1939, the station formed the intelligence staff of Army Kraków under General Antoni Szylling.


== Background and Kraków period (1923–1930) ==
Following the Peace of Riga and the conclusion of the border conflicts that defined the boundaries of the Second Polish Republic, the Polish General Staff reorganized its military intelligence apparatus in 1923. While the primary operational effort of the Second Department was initially weighted toward the Soviet Union (Vector "East"), the strategic necessity of monitoring German border garrisons, demilitarization compliance, and clandestine paramilitary formations along Poland's western and southwestern borders led to the creation of dedicated territorial stations (Ekspozytury).
Station No. 4 was established on 10 September 1923 in Kraków, with Captain Karol Franciszek Glazur appointed as its first commanding officer. Headquartered at ul. św. Marka 31 and later on ul. Warszawska, the station was assigned operational oversight over the southern frontier, facing German Silesia and the northern border of Czechoslovakia. In interwar Polish intelligence terminology, the station was classified as an organ of "shallow intelligence" (wywiad płytki). Unlike "deep intelligence" (wywiad głęboki), which was directed from the central headquarters in Warsaw and focused on strategic political decision-making, foreign ministries, and military high commands, shallow intelligence focused on an operational belt 100 to 150 km deep. Its principal objectives included:

Tracking the garrison deployments, movements, and peacetime strengths of the German Reichswehr in Silesia;
Monitoring cross-border transportation infrastructure, railway marshaling yards, and communication junctions;
Observing German paramilitary and veteran organizations, such as the Stahlhelm and the Freikorps successors;
Establishing covert observation posts along the frontier in cooperation with local customs, police, and from 1928, the newly established Border Guard (Straż Graniczna).
During its Kraków period, the post was commanded sequentially by Captain Glazur (1923–1925), Major Adam Studencki (1925–1927), Major August Nowosielski (1927–1928), and Major Ludwik Lucjan Sadowski (1928–1930).


== Relocation to Katowice (1930–1935) ==
In September 1930, the Second Department enacted a major structural reorganization of its western intelligence network. While Kraków possessed excellent administrative connections and lay safely behind the frontier, its geographical distance from the border impeded real-time tactical reconnaissance and swift agent handling. Consequently, Station No. 4 was moved directly to Katowice, the administrative and economic center of the Autonomous Silesian Voivodeship.
The transfer placed the station immediately adjacent to the international boundary running through the industrial conurbation of Upper Silesia. Operating from offices located at ul. Powstańców 31 (and later at ul. Chopina 8 and ul. Kościuszki 6) under various civilian and commercial covers, the Katowice branch gained direct access to cross-border commuter traffic, mining syndicates, regional railway lines connecting Katowice with Gleiwitz, Beuthen, and Oppeln, and a bilingual borderland population.
To establish comprehensive surveillance over frontier crossings and railway corridors, the station integrated personnel from the Border Guard. By 1934, two specialized Officer Intelligence Posts (Posterunki Oficerskie, PO) composed of seconded Border Guard officers operated under the Katowice station:

Officer Post No. 3 in Katowice: Senior Commissioner Marian Skibiński and Commissioner Henryk Leśniewski;
Officer Post No. 5 in Poznań: Commissioner Adam Biedrzyński and Subcommissioner Bogusław Skrzetuski.
Between September 1931 and May 1935, the branch was commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Karol Piasecki, an experienced artillery and staff officer who reorganized the operational reporting system and increased cooperation with the regional counterespionage divisions of the State Police.


== Operational peak under Kazimierz Szpądrowski (1935–1939) ==
In June 1935, Major Kazimierz Szpądrowski, a certified General Staff officer who had previously directed the Intelligence Training Section (Referat Wyszkolenia Wywiadowczego) at the Second Department central headquarters in Warsaw, was appointed Chief of Station No. 4. Under Szpądrowski's tenure, the station experienced its most active and successful operational period, evolving from a regional frontier monitoring post into a central node of Polish tactical intelligence against the Wehrmacht.


=== Agent network expansion ===
Szpądrowski systematically restructured the station's human intelligence operations. Recognizing that the rapid expansion and motorization of the German armed forces required permanent sources inside German territory rather than casual border informants, he initiated rigorous vetting and recruitment protocols. 
While in November 1937 the station had only three mobilization agents stationed in Germany, by late 1938 its operational asset list numbered 62 general intelligence agents (agenci ogólni) and 28 mobilization agents (agenci mobilizacyjni). These recruited sources were embedded in German railway administrative directorates, industrial plants in Upper and Lower Silesia, municipal transport departments, and frontier postal offices, providing regular reporting on German military troop movements, mobilization timetables, and fuel depots.


=== Technical Bureau development ===
To ensure operational self-sufficiency and secure communications without relying on the central laboratories in Warsaw, Szpądrowski expanded the station's Technical Bureau (Referat Techniczny). By 1938, technical specialists accounted for approximately one-sixth of the station's entire establishment. The bureau was equipped with:

Modern microphotographic and forensic reproduction laboratories;
Workshops for fabricating forged identity documents, German border permits (Grenzscheine), and railway passes;
Chemical analysis facilities for detecting and producing secret inks and microscopic letter drops;
Specialized surveillance and radio-monitoring apparatus.


=== Major operations and intelligence successes ===
During Szpądrowski's command, Station No. 4 carried out several notable operational initiatives:

Operation "Per-Pethe" (1935): A double-agent inspirational operation directed against the Gestapo border post in Bytom (Grenzpolizeikommissariat Beuthen). By feeding controlled information to German intelligence handlers, the Polish station succeeded in mapping the internal organizational roster, operational methods, and local informant network of the Bytom Gestapo, effectively neutralizing several German counterintelligence operations in Silesia.
1:300,000 Operational Staging Map (1937): The station synthesized years of field reporting into a comprehensive operational map at 1:300,000 scale. The document accurately charted the German army's operational staging grounds (przedpole), pre-designated assembly areas, march axes, and concentration zones designated for use in a mobilization against southern Poland.
Oder Fortifications and Signal Infrastructure: In 1937–1938, the station's reconnaissance agents discovered and mapped secret German concrete fortification lines and bunker belts along the Oder River, particularly southeast of Nowa Sól (Neusalz). In addition, agents identified covert wartime military telephone switchboards and emergency communications cables installed inside civilian municipal administration buildings in German Oppeln Silesia, intended to serve as wartime field headquarters for advancing German armies.


=== Conflict over intelligence assessments (1938–1939) ===
Following the Munich Agreement in September 1938 and the German consolidation in the Sudetenland, Station No. 4 warned with increasing urgency of extensive German troop concentrations and offensive preparations aimed at southern Poland. 
Szpądrowski's blunt and pessimistic assessments regarding the speed of German military mobilization and the vulnerability of Poland's southern industrial basin clashed with the official strategic outlook maintained by the Chief of the Polish General Staff, Brigadier General Wacław Stachiewicz, who regarded them as alarmist. As a result of this friction, Szpądrowski was relieved of his post on 31 January 1939 and transferred to field duty as an artillery battalion commander in Częstochowa.


== Eve of World War II (February – August 1939) ==
On 1 February 1939, Major Stanisław Kuniczak, an experienced General Staff officer, assumed command of Station No. 4. 


=== Subordinate network in late 1938 ===
In late 1938, prior to the spring 1939 reorganizations, the station's subordinate field network comprised four fixed officer posts and two mobile intelligence groups:

Officer Post No. 1 in Cieszyn (Posterunek Oficerski Nr 1): Captain Julian Lesiński;
Officer Post No. 2 in Chorzów (Posterunek Oficerski Nr 2): Captain Kazimierz Szczepański;
Officer Post No. 3 in Leszno (Posterunek Oficerski Nr 3): Captain Bolesław Górnik;
Officer Post No. 4 in Ostrów Wielkopolski (Posterunek Oficerski Nr 4): Captain Józef Wiklicki;
Intelligence Group I (Grupa Wywiadowcza I): Captain Franciszek Kretkowski;
Intelligence Group II (Grupa Wywiadowcza II): Captain Mieczysław Kurkowski.


=== Realignments during the Czechoslovak crisis (March – June 1939) ===
The final months before the outbreak of war were characterized by intensive restructuring caused by the rapid deterioration of Poland's strategic situation. On 15 March 1939, Nazi Germany occupied the Czech rump state, establishing the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia, while the nominally independent Slovak Republic became a German client state. This development outflanked Poland's southern border, adding over 800 kilometers of exposed frontier directly vulnerable to German forces.
