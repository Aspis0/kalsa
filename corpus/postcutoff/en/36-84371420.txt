Amar Mukunda (born December 8, 1992) is an American engineer, violence-prevention worker, and politician from Maryland. A member of the Democratic Party, he is the party's nominee for the Maryland Senate seat in District 39, in northern Montgomery County. In his first run for public office, he defeated Nancy J. King, the Senate majority leader, in the June 2026 Democratic primary, a result that The Baltimore Banner called perhaps the biggest surprise of the state's primary season. No Republican filed for the seat, and he is expected to take office in January 2027.


== Early life and education ==
Mukunda was born and raised in Montgomery County, Maryland, the son of immigrants from India and Pakistan. He attended Montgomery County public schools. His parents ran a business in the 1990s that went bankrupt in the economic downturn following the September 11 attacks.
He graduated from Amherst College in 2015 and later earned a master's degree in energy and infrastructure engineering from Stanford University. Between the two degrees he held a Fulbright research fellowship in Switzerland.


== Career ==
According to his campaign, Mukunda started a coding-education company while in college and later sold it; he has described himself as having founded and exited two technology companies.
From 2019 to 2023, he worked at Roca, a community violence-intervention nonprofit, where he became assistant director at its Maryland operation. His work involved outreach to young men in Baltimore identified as being at high risk of involvement in shootings. In February 2023 he was featured in a CBS News Sunday Morning segment by Ted Koppel about Roca's approach to steering young men away from gun violence. Through Roca, he was also named a Bloomberg Fellow at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health.
Mukunda serves as a combat engineer in the United States Army Reserve.


== Political career ==


=== 2026 Maryland Senate campaign ===
Mukunda announced a run for the Maryland House of Delegates in District 39 on September 18, 2025. In November 2025, he dropped that bid and filed instead to challenge King for the district's Senate seat, citing frustration with the Senate's leadership. He criticized King and Senate President Bill Ferguson for opposing a mid-decade congressional redistricting plan before the 2026 election, and faulted Senate leaders for holding back progressive legislation on issues such as immigration enforcement and school meal funding. He said he would not seek the endorsement of the Democratic Socialists of America.
Mukunda was endorsed by the Maryland chapter of the Sierra Club and by Run for Something, among other groups. He ran without contributions from corporate PACs or lobbyists and was outspent by King.
In the Democratic primary on June 23, 2026, Mukunda won 48.9 percent of the vote to King's 37.2 percent, a margin of about 1,400 votes, with a third candidate, Destiny Drake West, receiving 14.0 percent. King had led the Senate's Democratic majority since 2020 and was the longest-serving state legislator from Montgomery County. Mukunda said his campaign had drawn on widespread voter frustration and described himself as a critic of the Senate's Democratic establishment, which he accused of not standing up forcefully enough to President Donald Trump. Senator Cheryl Kagan, who represents a neighboring district, said she hoped he would be ready to listen and learn as a first-time officeholder.
If elected in November 2026, Mukunda would be the youngest member of the Maryland Senate when sworn in.


== Political positions ==
Mukunda has described his platform in terms of four goals: strengthening democratic institutions, making the cost of living more affordable, keeping schools well funded and free of guns, and responding to artificial intelligence and climate change. He has called for large investments in public transit, such as Metro and rail, to ease congestion on Interstate 270, and has proposed "clean energy opportunity zones" to draw investment and jobs to the state.
