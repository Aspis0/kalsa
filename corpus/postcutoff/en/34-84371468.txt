Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple, formerly Hongwanji Buddhist Church of Los Angeles, also known as Nishi Hompa Hongwanji Los Angeles Betsuin, is a Japanese Buddhist temple located in Los Angeles, California. The temple's second location was the first Buddhist temple in Los Angeles, one of the biggest and most influential in the United States, and was declared a Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument in 1986 and is a contributing property in the Little Tokyo Historic District.


== Organization ==
Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple is part of the Jodo Shinshu Hongwanji-ha.


== History ==

In the early 1900s, Los Angeles County's Japanese population was estimated to be more than 15,000, located primarily around Little Tokyo. The first Japanese Buddhist group formed in the area was Rafu Bukkyokai in 1904, followed by Nanka Bukkyokai (Buddhist Association of Southern California) in 1905 and Chuo Bukkyokai in 1912. The groups soon began feuding, and so the Nishi Hongan-ji sent Reverend Shuye Sonoda, who after months of mediation merged the three groups. The merger was officially ratified on September 30, 1917; the name for this group was Hongwanji Buddhist Church of Los Angeles and their reverend was Reverend Koyu Uchida from Nanka Bukkyokai. A merger ceremony was held on April. 17, 1920.


=== First location ===
Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple was first located on the second floor of Yamato Hall on Jackson Street.


=== Second location ===

Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple's second location, 355–369 East First Street and 109–119 North Central Avenue in downtown Los Angeles, was designed by local architect Edgar Cline and built in 1925. It was the first Buddhist Temple in Los Angeles, one of the largest and most influential in the United States, and was dedicated with more than 1,000 in attendance on November 12, 1925. The temple was elevated to Betsuin status in 1931, the first temple in the mainland United States to do so. 
In 1934, the temple had 1,500 members and an operating budget of $14,000 ($336,940 in 2025). In 1942, the temple was closed and the staff excepting Reverend Julius Goldwater were forced into internment camps. While closed, the temple stored its interned members' household goods; it also served as a hostel after the internments were lifted. The temple reopened on September 24, 1945 and had 2300 members in 1955.
The temple moved to a new location in 1969 and this building was sold to the City of Los Angeles in 1973. The building was declared Los Angeles Historic Cultural Monument No. 313 on October 24, 1986 and listed as a contributing property in the National Register of Historic Places-listed Little Tokyo Historic District. 
The Japanese American National Museum moved into the building in 1992. The following year, museum received the National Preservation Honor Award for their work restoring the building.


==== Architecture and design ====
Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple's second location features an eclectic East Asian Exotic Revival design. The building consists of three sections that curve around a street corner, with the entrance located in a plaza and featuring a large cement karahafu that replicates Nishi Hongan-ji's imperial gateway. The temple's hondo decorations resemble Nijo Castle and Nishi Hongan-ji.


=== Current location ===
Motivated by rumors that the city was planning to widen First Street an amount that would cut into Hompa Hongwanji Buddhist Temple, the temple moved to a new location, 815 East First Street, in 1969. This building, designed by Tom Makino and built by Collins and McPherson, began construction on October 14, 1968 with a total construction cost of $1.025 million ($9 million in 2025) paid for by 1,103 donors. The temple was dedicated on November 16, 1969, with more than 2,000 in attendance. A Japanese garden was dedicated the following year.
A multi-purpose hall was added to the temple complex in 1976. A large bell tower was dedicated on the temple's 75th anniversary in 1979.
