## 1. Waiting for a decision: the delivery fix (4 commits, not pushed)

`26115e4`, `dc2c931`, `3e4a578` and `7175b22` sit on top of `origin/brain` `0f9ec9c`. My rerun on `7175b22`:
workspace 880/0/20 over 47, kalsa-brain 179/0/0, tsc empty, smoke 62.

What they fix:
- An acknowledged delivery is cleared from the phone's own store record, not from record 0, which is the host.
- An acknowledgement is spent whatever the desk's state, including a late ack after a retry or after another
  phone completed.
- It is spent even when the store cannot be read.
Before the fix, a restart or a forget rebuilt the acknowledged delivery as pending and could serve the same
seal again inside its window. Both of reviewer A's reproduced replays are closed in the running process. Reviewer B: FIT.
Still open, and declared in `7175b22`: if the store clear fails, whether the store cannot be READ or cannot be WRITTEN,
the in-memory pending is spent but the stored copy survives. A restart inside the window can then serve that
seal again.

Reviewer A stays NOT FIT on two points. Both are design choices, and both are declared in the commit messages:
1. **Write-then-ack.** The desk spends a delivery once its LOCAL write succeeds. Through `tailscale serve`, that
   write lands in the loopback proxy, not in the phone. If the tailnet drops right then, the phone never sees
   the 200, its same-token retry gets a mute 403, and it must pair again. The alternative is to count the first
   authenticated door request as the receipt. That is a design change: **owner's call.**
2. **A token reused on a new square** can have its new delivery cleared by a late ack of the old attempt. The UX
   session confirmed its client mints a fresh token per square, and pinned it with a test (kalsa-ux
   src/screens/PairingScreen.test.ts:230, commit 36cfd29b).
Push or not: **owner's call.**

## 3. Today's first real scan (Tailscale)

- APK: `/tmp/apk-night/kalsa-apk-arm64-v8a-9aee66061c9a79a3d70d1b80b995b63bf67a32ca/app-release.apk`, sha256
  `fca9628676c4b944a74cf329c7b36d4a263c173ca3c59594084df03984324480` (UX session, branch `ux-2026-09-21` at `9aee6606`).
- On the Mac: `tailscale serve --bg 8131` for the door, and `tailscale serve --bg --https=8443 8134` for the desk.
  8131 and 8134 are the PREFERRED ports. If one is taken, the app falls back to another port and the Devices page
  says "the pairing desk is on <n> this time". Use the numbers the page shows.
  `serve --bg` rules PERSIST, even across a reboot. Remove exactly these two after the test by appending `off`:
  `tailscale serve --bg 8131 off` and `tailscale serve --bg --https=8443 8134 off` (Tailscale serve docs).
  `tailscale serve reset` clears the whole serve config. On 2026-09-23 `tailscale serve status` said "No serve
  config", so reset would also be safe unless new rules are added. The CLI is
  /Applications/Tailscale.app/Contents/MacOS/Tailscale.
  The phone types https://marcos-macbook-pro.tail80a42d.ts.net and …ts.net:8443.
- The phone signs `reachable` EXACTLY as the square shows it (loopback). It never signs the tailnet URL it dials.
- Header: `Authorization: Bearer <64 lowercase hex>`.
- Before Allow, the door answers an empty 401 on purpose. Allow reaches the door on the ~1 s tick. A 401 that
  persists a few seconds after Allow points first at the header or credential, but it is a heuristic: a stale
  serve target or a failed reconcile also gives 401.
- A mute 403: compare the phone's diagnostic hex (payload, MAC, token, ciphertext) with the independent Python
  reference, ~/kalsa-qdc/pairing_vectors.py (copied from /tmp).
- A 403 on a retry right after a timeout is most likely the write-then-ack case above. Recover with "Pair
  another phone".
