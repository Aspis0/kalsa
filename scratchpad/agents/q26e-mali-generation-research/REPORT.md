# Mali generation research for GPU inference

**Verdict (22 Aug 2026):** The earliest sensible target is **Valhall 3rd generation: Mali-G610/G710**, not G57. G610 is the first mainstream Mali family with the doubled per-core FP32 issue rate (128 FP32 ops/clock/core versus 64 on G57/G68/G78), and G610 MC6/D8100–8200 or G710 MC10/D9000 have ample peak headroom over their CPUs. For a *credible* tuned LLM path, however, the safer first target is **G610 MC6 or newer**, with **G615/G715/G720** providing the strongest software/capability case. G610 phones were already around the $250–350 tier in 2023–24; G615/G720 reached roughly $250–350 in 2024–25. That makes waiting for G610+ hardware more rational than investing in generic G57 kernels now. The G57 result is not a paradox: its 128 GFLOPS-class peak is only a ceiling, and 20–22 GFLOPS q8 / 7.8 GFLOPS f32 is consistent with low occupancy, dequantization, global-memory traffic, launch overhead, and a driver that exposes subgroup features but does not make them reliably usable.

**Important subgroup qualification.** Arm’s OpenCL guide says `cl_khr_subgroups` is supported on Bifrost from G76 onward and on all Valhall/5th-Gen GPUs, and the extension covers shuffle, broadcast, vote, ballot, arithmetic and reductions: [OpenCL guide](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/64634b023e01ef4d7cf6c183). That is an API/driver capability statement, not proof that every Android binary is correct or fast. The observed G57 `sub_group_shuffle` SIGILL should therefore be treated as a device-driver/implementation blocker; use a runtime probe and a no-subgroup fallback. G610 still reports a preferred subgroup multiple of 16 in Arm’s forum example: [G610 subgroup multiple](https://community.arm.com/forums/f/high-performance-computing-forum/52533/how-to-change-value-cl_kernel_preferred_work_group_size_multiple-for-opencl-mali-platform).

Arm classifies **G615/G715 as 4th-generation Valhall**; **G720/G625/G725 are 5th Gen**. G310/G510 are lower-end Valhall derivatives and can have ranges of core configurations; they do not alter the G57→G610→G715 arithmetic-width progression. Arm’s comparison sheet notes that G6 products use the same core specifications as the corresponding G7 products, but core count and clocks are SoC choices.

## 2. Representative SoC peaks versus CPU

Formula: `GPU FP32 peak = Arm FP32 ops/core/clock × cores × GPU clock`; FP16 is 2×. Frequencies are typical shipping configurations and can be DVFS-limited. CPU figures are **planning estimates for sustained all-core FP32 FMA**, not vendor benchmarks: roughly 45–75% of the ideal NEON FMA ceiling, anchored to the measured G99 8-thread result of ~70 GFLOPS. They are useful for threshold decisions, not a claim of reproducible performance. GPU data points are cross-checkable in the [MediaTek SoC list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_MediaTek_systems_on_chips) and official product pages such as [D700](https://i.mediatek.com/dimensity-700), [D810](https://i.mediatek.com/dimensity-810), [D900](https://i.mediatek.com/dimensity-900), [D1080](https://i.mediatek.com/dimensity-1080), [D7200](https://i.mediatek.com/hubfs/Dimensity-7200-CP-LP/Dimensity_7200_Infographic.pdf?hsLang=en), [D8100/8200](https://i.mediatek.com/dimensity-8200), and [D8300](https://www.mediatek.com/products/smartphones/mediatek-dimensity-8300).

The raw crossover is therefore earlier than the *software* crossover. G57 and G68 already have more theoretical FP32 than the CPU, but only by 1.5–3× and with much worse usable-kernel efficiency in this workload. G610 MC4 is the first plausible target where a tuned prefill GEMM can leave a large enough margin for memory traffic, dequantization and dispatch overhead; G610 MC6/G615 MC6 is the better commercial target.

## 3. Evidence that Mali inference works

| Evidence | Mali generation used | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Arm NN/Arm Compute Library OpenCL work | G77/G78 and earlier Mali families | Tuned LWS/cache-aware kernels matter: Arm reports a 1.12–1.8× tuner uplift and describes G77 as a 60% ML improvement over G76. This is CNN/NN evidence, not LLM proof: [Arm NN tuner](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/ai-blog/posts/arm-nn-gpu-inference-with-opencl-tuner). |
| ML Drift, CVPRW 2025 | Mali-G715 in Google Pixel 9 and Immortalis-G720 in Vivo X100 Pro | Strongest published LLM evidence found. Across the Mali test set, ML Drift reported Llama 3.2 3B about **791 prefill tok/s, 12.5 decode tok/s** with q8, versus MLC about **89.2 / 11.2** with q4f16; the paper says its Mali results were comparable to MLC and that prefill was compute-bound. [Paper, open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/EDGE/papers/Tang_Scaling_On-Device_GPU_Inference_for_Large_Generative_Models_CVPRW_2025_paper.pdf). |
| llama.cpp Vulkan field report | Mali-G715, Pixel 9 Pro XL | A non-peer-reviewed report gets ~7–8 tok/s on Gemma 2 2B versus ~1–2 tok/s CPU-only, and claims 12–15 tok/s after GPU-frequency tuning. Useful feasibility evidence, not a controlled benchmark: [llama.cpp discussion](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23193). |
| LiteRT / Google AI Edge model report | Mali-G715, Pixel 8a / Tensor G3 | Reports int4 beating int8 on GPU by 1.8× while CPU int8 wins some prefill cases; this reinforces that quantization layout and stage matter: [Granite LiteRT report](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/granite-4.1-3b). |
| MNN/NCNN/MLC | Broad OpenCL/Vulkan support, but public Mali LLM numbers are sparse | MNN documents OpenCL/Vulkan GPU backends, and MLC has Mali builds, but I found no controlled G57/G610 phone result that demonstrates CPU-beating LLM prefill. Treat framework support as portability evidence, not performance evidence: [MNN](https://github.com/alibaba/MNN), [MLC Android deployment](https://github.com/mlc-ai/mlc-llm/blob/main/docs/deploy/android.rst). |
| Arm KleidiAI | CPU, not Mali GPU | KleidiAI is explicitly Arm CPU/NEON/SVE/SME micro-kernels. It helps explain why the CPU baseline keeps improving; it is not evidence for a Mali path: [KleidiAI](https://github.com/ARM-software/kleidiai), [Arm Kleidi](https://developer.arm.com/ai/kleidi-libraries). |

Public evidence thus jumps from “tuned CNN kernels can work on Mali” to “LLM prefill works well on G715/G720.” There is a conspicuous evidence gap for G57/G68 and even G610 phones. The lack of a report is not proof of impossibility, but it makes a G57-specific kernel investment a poor bet.

**Timing call:** G610 MC4/MC6 is already in the target market’s upper half; G615 MC6 and G720 MC7 reached roughly the $250–300 value tier during 2024–25 and should diffuse further through 2026. G625/G725 are Arm’s lower/premium 5th-Gen licensing options, but there is not yet enough evidence of broad Africa/SEA phone volume at $100–250. Tecno/Infinix/itel are especially likely to stay on G57-class or non-Mali/entry GPU parts at the bottom of the range; Xiaomi/POCO and some Samsung/Exynos models are the practical way to reach G610+.

## Bottom line

The **earliest Mali generation worth a tuned-kernel GPU experiment is Valhall 3rd Gen (G610/G710)**: examples are Dimensity 8100/8200 (G610 MC6), Dimensity 9000 (G710 MC10), and phones such as Infinix GT 20 Pro, POCO/Xiaomi performance models, and older D9000 flagships. G615/G715/G720 are better still—G615 MC6 in POCO X6 Pro and G720 MC7 in POCO X7 Pro are already near the target price ceiling—and they add a second large FMA jump, better subgroup execution, and (G715 family) matrix-multiply support. For the Africa/SEA $100–180 volume tier, G57-class phones will remain dominant, but they are the wrong optimization target for this project: the measured 20–22 GFLOPS q8 and 7.8 GFLOPS f32 already show the practical ceiling is below the 8-thread CPU. Park G57/G68; revisit when a representative G610 MC6 or G615 MC6 phone is available, and treat G715/G720 as the first tier with published LLM-prefill evidence and the strongest plausibility of beating its same-SoC CPU once tuned.
