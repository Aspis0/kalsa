# iOS local-model readiness research

**Research date: 2026-10-01.** The iOS 27 / WWDC26 information below reflects Apple documentation available on this date. Citations include the source date (or “checked 2026-10-01” for living documentation) and a short supporting quote. **UNVERIFIED** means I did not find a sufficiently authoritative, explicit answer.

## 1. Apple Foundation Models

- **Availability and hardware:** The framework is a native Swift API, introduced for iOS 26, and requires Apple Intelligence availability. Apple’s current supported iPhones are iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max and iPhone 16 or later; the feature also requires a supported language/region and the model may still be downloading. (Apple Developer, checked 2026-10-01: “Model availability depends on whether the device and region supports Apple Intelligence.” [Generating content and performing tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-content-and-performing-tasks-with-foundation-models?changes=_1); Apple Support, checked 2026-10-01: “iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, or iPhone 16 model or later.” [iPhone requirements](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-started-with-apple-intelligence-iphc28624b81/27/ios/27))
- **Model size:** The WWDC25 model was about 3B parameters at 2-bit weights. Apple’s WWDC26 talk says iOS 27 has a new model “rebuilt from the ground up” but does not disclose its parameter count or quantization. **Current iOS 27 model size: UNVERIFIED.** (Apple WWDC25, June 9, 2025: “3 billion parameters, each quantized to 2 bits.” [Meet Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/); Apple WWDC26, June 2026: “a new on-device model, rebuilt from the ground up.” [What’s new](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/))
- **Context:** In iOS 26.0–26.3, Apple documented 4,096 tokens per session; iOS 26.4 added `contextSize` and token-count APIs. On iOS 27, query the model at runtime rather than baking in a number. Apple’s 2025 pretraining sequences up to 65K do **not** mean the exposed session accepts 65K. (Apple TN3193, updated March 31, 2026: “context window of 4096 tokens”; [Managing the context window](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window?language=_8); Apple Foundation Models updates, February 2026: “Use the `contextSize` property to get the maximum context size”; [updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/foundationmodels))
- **Languages:** The current Apple Intelligence list includes English, French, Italian, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese, so all five Kalsa product languages are listed. Availability is conditioned on device and Siri language matching; Foundation Models checks prompt language and can return unsupported-language errors. This establishes listed support, not equal quality in all five languages. (Apple Support, checked 2026-10-01: “English … French … Italian … Spanish … Chinese (simplified), Chinese (traditional)”; [languages and regions](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/121115); Apple Developer, checked 2026-10-01: “checks the language or languages of the input prompt text”; [Foundation Models language support](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models?changes=_10_5))
- **Chat/reasoning/tools/structured output/streaming:** It supports multi-turn stateful sessions, streaming, guided generation (`@Generable` constrained output), and developer-defined tools with serial/parallel calls. Apple describes the iOS 27 model as better at logic and tool calling, but still publishes no system-model “thinking” trace or reasoning-effort control. Reasoning levels are documented for Apple’s 32K Private Cloud Compute model, which is not local. **Visible on-device chain-of-thought/reasoning mode: UNVERIFIED.** (Apple WWDC25, June 9, 2025: “guided generation,” “streaming APIs,” and “tool calling”; [session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/); Apple ML Research, July 17, 2025: “parallel and serial tool calls”; [2025 model report](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates); Apple WWDC26, June 2026: on-device model “better at logic and tool calling,” while “reasoning level” is described for PCC; [framework updates](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/))
- **Vision:** The iOS 27 Foundation Models session accepts image attachments in prompts. Apple’s 2025 tech report also described on-device visual understanding, but the framework’s image-prompt API is specifically a WWDC26 addition. (Apple WWDC26, June 2026: “the on-device model is also gaining Vision capabilities”; [framework updates](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/))
- **Region:** In the current Apple Support page, Apple Intelligence works in most regions and remains available while traveling. EU residents have access to the next-generation features on supported devices; in mainland China, Apple Intelligence “will not currently work” under the stated purchase/account-location conditions. Recheck before launch because these are live regional policies. (Apple Support, checked 2026-10-01: “available in most regions”; “available for users based in the EU”; “will not currently work” in mainland China; [availability](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/121115))
- **Quality versus 3–4B open models:** Apple’s 2025 human-evaluation report says its then-current on-device model performed favorably against Qwen2.5-3B across evaluated languages and was competitive with Qwen3-4B and Gemma3-4B in English. That is Apple’s own evaluation, of its 2025 model; it does not establish quality for the rebuilt iOS 27 model, nor a broad win on coding/math/chat. **Independent, current iOS 27 model comparison: UNVERIFIED.** (Apple ML Research, July 17, 2025: “favorably against … Qwen-2.5-3B across all languages” and “competitive against … Qwen-3-4B and Gemma-3-4B in English”; [evaluation](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates))
- **Restrictions:** The Foundation Models Acceptable Use Requirements prohibit, among other things, circumvention of safety controls and generating/reproducing Apple’s model training data. App Review guideline 2.5.2 prohibits downloading/installing/executing code that changes app functionality; guideline 2.5.4 limits background services to their intended uses. (Apple Acceptable Use Requirements, checked 2026-10-01: “Circumvents any safety policies, guardrails, or restrictions”; [terms](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/acceptable-use-requirements-for-the-foundation-models-framework/); Apple App Review Guidelines, checked 2026-10-01: “may not download, install, or execute code that introduces or changes features”; [2.5.2–2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/uk/))
- **React Native / Expo:** Community wrappers exist, including `react-native-apple-llm` and `@ratley/react-native-apple-foundation-models`; the latter documents Expo prebuild/rebuild and iOS 26+ Apple Intelligence hardware. They bridge the Swift framework and cannot remove Apple’s device, locale, or OS limits. The first project labels itself beta. **Long-term maintenance, production support, and Kalsa compatibility are UNVERIFIED; treat as integration candidates, not an Apple-supported RN API.** (Repository, checked 2026-10-01: “This is a beta feature”; [react-native-apple-llm](https://github.com/deveix/react-native-apple-llm); repository, checked 2026-10-01: “run `npx expo prebuild` and rebuild your iOS app”; [Expo wrapper](https://github.com/ratley/react-native-apple-foundation-models); Apple framework documentation calls this a “Swift API”; [WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/286/))
- **WWDC26 / adapters:** iOS 27’s meaningful changes are a new on-device model, image input, context inspection/token counting, and a model-provider abstraction (including Core AI and MLX providers), plus Private Cloud Compute as a 32K reasoning-capable alternative. The old custom Foundation Models adapter-training toolkit is **not** a forward path: Apple labels version 26.0.0 its last release and says it is incompatible with iOS 27+. Do not plan a new supported system-model LoRA workflow. (Apple WWDC26, June 2026: “LanguageModel protocol”; [session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/); Apple Adapter Toolkit, checked 2026-10-01: “26.0.0 is the last release … not compatible with … iOS … 27 and later”; [toolkit notice](https://developer.apple.com/kr/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/))

## 2. Open models and iPhone runtimes

- **Runtime reality:** llama.cpp has a working iOS Metal path; MLX Swift has iOS support; Apple’s iOS 27 Core AI is now Apple’s first-party deployment path for selected on-device models and plugs into Foundation Models sessions. Core ML remains available for converted models, but ANE execution depends on conversion/graph support and is not automatic for arbitrary GGUF. **A universal “ANE is faster” claim is false.** (llama.cpp iPhone benchmark table, checked 2026-10-01: “A18 Pro … Metal”; [results](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/4508); Apple WWDC26: same `LanguageModelSession` API with Core AI-backed models; [Core AI session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/326/); Apple ML Research: Core ML blends CPU, GPU and ANE “if available”; [ANE transformer deployment](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers))
- **Representative measured throughput (not promises):** On iPhone 17 Pro/A19 Pro, an independent short-chat test of ~4-bit models (128 output tokens, median of 3 cold runs) reported Qwen3.5 2B at 61.2 tok/s with MLX Swift, 39.1 with llama.cpp, and 27.9 with CoreML/ANE. Gemma 4 E2B measured 47.5, 37.8, and 33.4 tok/s respectively (LiteRT-LM was 55.4). This is one author’s early benchmark; rerun on Kalsa’s build, prompts, thermal state, and models. (John Rocky, 2026, checked 2026-10-01: “iPhone 17 Pro (A19 Pro / iOS 26.4.2),” “median of 3 cold runs”; [runtime comparison](https://dev.to/john-rocky/on-device-llm-on-iphone-which-runtime-is-fastest-mlx-vs-llamacpp-vs-litert-lm-vs-coreml-1b42))
- **Other measured reference points:** llama.cpp’s A17 Pro iPhone 15 Pro Max table reports 1.1B Q4 at 54.95 tok/s; its A18 Pro iPhone 16 Pro table reports 1.1B Q4 at 69.77 tok/s and 7.24B Q4 at 11.64 tok/s (Metal, 128-token decode test). A separate iOS Q4_K_M benchmark across four models reports 7.6–11.8 decode tok/s; device/method comparability is limited. I did not find a sufficiently documented exact 8B Q4 iPhone measurement: **UNVERIFIED**. These differences show why model, runtime, quant, context, and thermal conditions must accompany every speed number. (llama.cpp discussion, checked 2026-10-01: A17 Pro “llama 1B Q4_0 … tg 128 54.95”; A18 Pro “llama 7B Q4_0 … tg 128 11.64”; [benchmark](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/4508); iOS benchmark repo, checked 2026-10-01: “1,000 prompts … Q4_K_M … Metal”; [benchmark results](https://github.com/singhh5050/ios-llm-benchmarking-results))
- **Core ML / Core AI / ANE:** Apple’s 2026 Core AI SDK supports model bundles (`.aimodel`), model specialization and profiling. A community CoreML-LLM implementation demonstrates ANE-first chunked inference and low resident memory for certain models, but it is not Apple’s official sample and model-specific. Apple’s Core AI talk discusses specialization latency; plan for first-load compilation and cache. **A blanket published Apple claim that ANE performs LLM prefill better than Metal: UNVERIFIED.** (Apple Core AI docs, checked 2026-10-01: “app needs access to the `.aimodel` file at runtime”; [integration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/CoreAI/integrating-on-device-ai-models-in-your-app-with-core-ai?changes=l_4); Apple WWDC26: specialization “can take a significant amount of time”; [Core AI integration](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/326/); community project, checked 2026-10-01: “ANE-first”; [CoreML-LLM](https://github.com/john-rocky/CoreML-LLM))
- **Memory and mmap:** Apple exposes `increased-memory-limit` and `extended-virtual-addressing` entitlements; the latter expands address space and Apple specifically cites memory-mapping assets streamed to GPU. Apple does not publish a fixed per-app jetsam/RSS allocation table for 8GB versus 12GB iPhones. A 2026 Apple Developer Forums report observes about a 6GB ceiling even with both entitlements, but this is one developer’s measurement, not an Apple guarantee. **Exact 8GB/12GB per-app ceilings and entitlement approval/benefit: UNVERIFIED.** (Apple entitlement docs, checked 2026-10-01: “larger addressable space”; “memory map assets to stream to the GPU”; [extended virtual addressing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.extended-virtual-addressing?changes=__2%2C__2&language=objc%2Cobjc); Apple forum, 2026, checked 2026-10-01: “per-app memory ceiling of about 6 GB”; [reported measurement](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834805))
- **Background execution:** Ordinary apps are suspended shortly after backgrounding; a UIKit background task provides time for critical work, and iOS has a user-initiated `BGContinuedProcessingTask` for jobs taking minutes or more. It is not a general promise that chat generation can keep running indefinitely after the user leaves. Design generation as foreground work, save state, and support cancellation/resume. (Apple Developer, checked 2026-10-01: “Shortly after … returns, the system puts your app into the suspended state”; [background execution](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/extending-your-app-s-background-execution-time?changes=_8); Apple BackgroundTasks docs, checked 2026-10-01: continuous task “starts in the foreground” and must respond to user action; [long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/BackgroundTasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados?changes=lat_7__3&language=objc))

## 3. Model shortlist for the five languages

- **Assessment (not a five-language head-to-head result): best first general model to test is Qwen3.5-4B (or Qwen3-4B as a stable baseline).** Qwen3-4B advertises 100+ languages, thinking/non-thinking mode, tool use, 32K native context, and Apache-2.0. Qwen3.5-4B is listed under Apache-2.0 and is multimodal, but verify exact GGUF/llama.cpp support, vision path, and release revisions before shipping. This most directly covers EN/IT/FR/ES/ZH plus reasoning and tools. (Qwen3 model card, May 2025: “100+ languages”; “32,768 natively”; “Apache-2.0”; [Qwen3-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B); Qwen3.5 model card, checked 2026-10-01: “License: apache-2.0”; [Qwen3.5-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B))
- **Best low-memory text baseline: LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct / Thinking.** It has a small footprint and both instruct and thinking variants, but its listed eight languages are EN, AR, ZH, FR, DE, JA, KO, ES: **Italian is absent**. Its license is LFM Open License 1.0, Apache-derived but commercial use is restricted for companies at or above $10M annual revenue unless separately licensed. LFM2.5-VL is a vision option; its separate model card/license must be checked. (Liquid model card, checked 2026-10-01: “Languages: English, Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Spanish”; [LFM2.5 1.2B](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct); Liquid license, checked 2026-10-01: commercial-use threshold “$10 million USD”; [LFM Open License](https://www.liquid.ai/lfm-license))
- **Best mobile multimodal fallback to test: Gemma 3n E2B.** Google says >140 languages, image/audio/video input, 32K context, and effective memory load about 1.91B with parameter skipping/PLE caching, but the full E2B model ordinarily loads >5B parameters. It has open weights under Google Gemma terms (not Apache/MIT). **Exact per-language parity for Kalsa’s five languages and stock llama.cpp performance: UNVERIFIED.** (Google Gemma 3n docs, checked 2026-10-01: “trained in over 140 languages”; “effective memory load of just under 2 billion (1.91B)”; [model overview](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/gemma-3n); [model card and terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/gemma-3n/model_card))
- **Gemma 4:** Google announced Gemma 4 in April 2026 under Apache-2.0. The mobile-sized E2B is a strong candidate for iPhone benchmarking; larger E4B may fit only on more capable devices depending on quant/runtime/context. Do not confuse the Gemma 4 26B-A4B model’s ~4B active parameters with a 4B memory model: its full expert weights are 26B. (Google Developers Blog, April 2, 2026: “available under the Apache 2.0 license”; [Gemma 4](https://developers.googleblog.com/bring-state-of-the-art-agentic-skills-to-the-edge-with-gemma-4/); Google DeepMind, checked 2026-10-01: “Gemma 4 26B-A4B”; [model family](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-4/))
- **Phi-4-mini-instruct:** 3.8B dense, 128K advertised context, function calling, broad language list including all five target languages, MIT license; however, Microsoft warns it is trained primarily in English and other-language quality is worse. Its published full-precision checkpoint is 7.69GB, so use a tested mobile quantization. Good reasoning/math/code comparator, lower multilingual confidence than Qwen/Gemma. (Microsoft model card, February 2025: “3.8B parameters”; “128K token context”; languages include “Chinese … French … Italian … Spanish”; “MIT”; [Phi-4-mini](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-mini-instruct); same card: “Languages other than English will experience worse performance”; [limitations](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-mini-instruct))
- **OpenELM:** Apple’s open model family includes 1.1B-class checkpoints and public training/inference code, but it is an older text-only research baseline and not the Apple Intelligence model. The released checkpoint uses Apple Sample Code License, not Apache/MIT; assess those terms before product distribution. No current evidence found that it beats the above candidates on Kalsa’s five-language chat/tool/code workload. (Apple OpenELM model card, checked 2026-10-01: “apple-sample-code-license”; [OpenELM 1.1B Instruct](https://huggingface.co/apple/OpenELM-1_1B-Instruct); Apple CoreNet, checked 2026-10-01: OpenELM is a listed research project; [CoreNet](https://github.com/apple/corenet))
- **MoE with ~3B active on an 8GB iPhone:** Active parameters describe per-token compute, not total expert weights that must be available. For example, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B has 35B total / 3B active and Apache-2.0; loading the whole quantized expert set plus KV/cache/runtime into an 8GB phone is not realistic as an ordinary resident model. Kalsa’s expert streaming may change the feasibility, but requires its own actual-device tests and storage/memory accounting. **That a ~3B-active MoE will run acceptably on 8GB iPhone with Kalsa expert streaming: UNVERIFIED.** (Qwen model license, checked 2026-10-01: “Apache License, Version 2.0”; [Qwen3.5-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B); Apple memory-entitlement documentation notes that mapping/streaming assets is an address-space use case, not a guarantee of resident model capacity; [entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.extended-virtual-addressing?changes=__2%2C__2&language=objc%2Cobjc))

## 4. Model distribution

- **Weights after install:** Guideline 2.5.2 clearly bars downloaded executable code that changes app functionality. Apple does not explicitly classify LLM weights as “data” or rule on apps that download arbitrary model weights. If the app ships a fixed inference engine and weights are only parameters/data, that distinction is plausible but **App Review treatment is UNVERIFIED**; do not claim guaranteed approval. Keep executable/runtime logic in the reviewed app and make model choice/download transparent. (Apple App Review Guidelines, checked 2026-10-01: “may not download, install, or execute code that introduces or changes features”; [guideline 2.5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/uk/))
- **Apple-hosted assets:** On-demand Resources are deprecated beginning iOS 27; Apple recommends Background Assets. Legacy iOS 18+ ODR limits were 8GB per thinned asset pack and 70GB hosted total. Background Assets supports essential, prefetch, and on-demand asset packs; current exact Background Assets total/per-pack caps for model libraries are **UNVERIFIED** in this research. (App Store Connect, checked 2026-10-01: “deprecated … as of iOS 27”; “8GB” per pack and “70GB” hosted; [ODR limits](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-uploads/on-demand-resources-size-limits/); Apple Background Assets docs, checked 2026-10-01: “downloads essential asset packs … prefetches others … on demand”; [Background Assets](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/BackgroundAssets?language=_1))
- **Install size:** Apple says Background Assets can host larger assets separately from the build; it does not mean the models are free of download/storage constraints. iOS 27 Apple Intelligence’s own downloaded system models use up to 8GB on most supported devices and up to 14GB on specified 12GB-memory devices, a reminder that system storage budgets are material. (App Store Connect, checked 2026-10-01: Background Assets “host larger assets … separately from your build”; [maximum build sizes](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-uploads/maximum-build-file-sizes/); Apple Support, checked 2026-10-01: “Up to 14 GB” / “Up to 8 GB”; [system requirements](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/121115))

## 5. Bottom line

**iOS is easier to make consistent, but not categorically easier for local LLMs.** Inference: one public Metal GPU API/vendor reduces Android’s hardware/driver variance; Apple’s unified-memory GPU model lets CPU and GPU share system memory. Apple’s Metal, MLX Swift, and now Core AI provide strong native paths. (Apple Metal docs, checked 2026-10-01: “Apple GPUs have a unified memory model in which the CPU and the GPU share system memory”; [resource storage modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/choosing-a-resource-storage-mode-for-apple-gpus?changes=_2&language=objc)) Apple supplies a polished private model/API for supported devices. Hard parts are tight and unpublished per-process memory limits, model weights and KV competing for shared RAM, foreground/background suspension, OS-version and region/device eligibility, Swift-to-React-Native bridging, Core ML/Core AI model conversion, and App Review uncertainty around post-install weight downloads. An 8GB iPhone should target 1–4B quantized models; treat 8B as device/context-specific, not a baseline. A 12GB model class gives more headroom but is not a promise of 12GB app RSS. **“Much easier than Android” is true for one predictable Apple GPU stack; false if it means fewer distribution, memory, or lifecycle constraints.** (Apple WWDC26, June 2026: Core AI supplies “a modern, memory-safe Swift API” and “fine-grained control over inference memory”; [Core AI](https://developer.apple.com/core-ai/); Apple App Review 2.5.2 and Apple background-task documentation cited above; Apple MLX/llama.cpp iPhone throughput comparisons cited in §2.)

### Five decision-relevant facts

1. Apple FM is a useful zero-download fallback, supports the five target languages in the current language list, but only on Apple Intelligence-capable devices and in available regions. [Apple device/language support](https://support.apple.com/en-asia/121115) — checked 2026-10-01, “iPhone 15 Pro … or iPhone 16 model or later”; “Italian … Spanish … Chinese.”
2. iOS 27 replaces the old Foundation Model and removes the old adapter toolkit; use runtime `contextSize`, do not plan system-model LoRA shipping. [Apple WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/) — June 2026, “new on-device model”; [adapter toolkit](https://developer.apple.com/kr/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/) — checked 2026-10-01, “not compatible with … iOS … 27 and later.”
3. For the first open-model benchmark matrix, use Qwen3.5-4B/Qwen3-4B, Gemma 3n E2B, and LFM2.5 1.2B; LFM2.5’s supported list omits Italian and its commercial license has a $10M revenue threshold. [Qwen3-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B), [Gemma 3n](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/gemma-3n), [LFM2.5](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct), [LFM license](https://www.liquid.ai/lfm-license) — checked 2026-10-01; quotes in §3.
4. Current community measurement on iPhone 17 Pro puts Qwen3.5 2B at 61.2 tok/s MLX Swift versus 39.1 llama.cpp and 27.9 CoreML/ANE; validate with Kalsa’s exact build and thermal workload. [Runtime benchmark](https://dev.to/john-rocky/on-device-llm-on-iphone-which-runtime-is-fastest-mlx-vs-llamacpp-vs-litert-lm-vs-coreml) — 2026, “median of 3 cold runs.”
5. Don’t assume an 8GB/12GB RAM spec equals available app memory; Apple publishes entitlement descriptions, not fixed jetsam budgets, and backgrounding can suspend inference. [Memory entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.extended-virtual-addressing?changes=__2%2C__2&language=objc%2Cobjc), [background limits](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/extending-your-app-s-background-execution-time?changes=_8) — checked 2026-10-01; quotes in §2.
