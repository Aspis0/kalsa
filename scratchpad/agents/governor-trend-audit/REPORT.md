2. **CONFIRMED (quantization arithmetic; excursion distinction is absent by design).** With 0.1 C quantization, two readings of a constant underlying temperature can differ by at most one 0.1 C step from endpoint quantization. Across the 60 s minimum span, that is `0.1 C / 1 min = 0.1 C/min`; `1.5 / 0.1 = 15`, so the claimed factor is correct for quantization alone. A real 1.5 C rise in 60 s is exactly `1.5 C/min`; the endpoint estimator does not distinguish that excursion from a sustained ramp or a bad sensor outlier. The engine compares with `>= 1.5` and, while WARM and at the associated temperature condition, enters COOLMODE (`kalsallama` pin `1577495b92096fca572f57a35b71607bacac85d5`, `src/llama-governor-policy.cpp:13,140-143`). That is consistent with the engine's rate threshold if the observed endpoint average is the intended signal. Quantization by itself cannot create that excursion from a flat sensor, but the estimate can move by 0.1 C/min around a real near-threshold rate. The 30 s test uses a 0.9 C change, which is 9 quantization steps, so it demonstrates the span gate, not a one-step quantization artifact (`governorTrend.test.ts:31-35`).

## Could the governor act on a number that is not true?

Yes, conditionally. On the ordinary battery path, it receives a finite endpoint average calculated from real app observations; a flat signal cannot cross 1.5 C/min from 0.1 C quantization alone. But the app does not detect spikes or distinguish an abrupt real excursion from a sustained rise, it can bridge a sensor-invalid interval, and a finite bench-injected trend bypasses the producer. Thus the value can be mathematically valid for two endpoints while not describing the current rate, or explicitly injected rather than measured. The engine's WARM rule can act on such a value when its temperature condition also holds.

## Is the SEVERE stop correct, and what does completion cost?

The stop is correct: `platform_thermal` is present in the app payload but absent from the pinned binding parser and engine struct, so it dies at the JSI parse boundary. To make SEVERE affect a governor decision requires coordinated changes in three repos: define a canonical platform-state field and decision semantics plus tests in `kalsallama`; teach `llama.rn-kalsa` to parse and type it while updating its embedded engine pin and `vendor/VERSIONS`; then bump the app's binding pin and lockfile. The pin/build chain is engine ref -> binding vendored engine and `VERSIONS` -> app `package.json` and lockfile -> `scripts/assert-engine-provenance.sh` -> APK built from that pinned state. The app already transports the raw value, but engine policy and cross-platform representation still need design and pin tests.
